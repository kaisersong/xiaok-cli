# Intent dependency test observation windows

The observer changes documented here affect tests only. The later, independently reviewed receiver product change is documented in [receiver liveness](1.6.4-receiver-liveness-design.md). It creates no Source, scope, authority, Native qualification or physical-close proof.

The original release plan explicitly permits quality to determine reasonable CI runner budgets. On the unchanged original Intent test file, local Node 24 completed 22 cases with one existing opt-in skip in 759 seconds. Hosted macOS Node 24 cancelled eight cases at their 30–50-second Vitest windows; the first case took about 34.6 seconds locally versus 49.3 seconds in CI against its 45-second runner limit. The preserved logs are failures, not passes.

The case observer cancellation is separate from the original qualified authority clock, material expiry and transport limits. `f.setTime(10000)`, material `expiresAt:105000`, original Current/whole-population checks, signals, late-error propagation and real cleanup joins remain unchanged. RPC limits were unchanged by these test-observer changes; the separate receiver-only product liveness refinement uses 30 seconds while the generic IPC default remains 15 seconds. `onTestFinished` still waits for the original scenario. Unknown cleanup never becomes a successful completion.

Only cases that construct the full fixture inside their bodies use a reviewed macOS/Linux observer minimum: 300 seconds (hosted full-fixture observations reached 65–95 seconds under the earlier 60/75/90-second limits). Other operating systems retain their original budgets. Longer original budgets remain, including the one-hour opt-in case. The five invocation cases already construct their fixtures in `beforeEach`; their case budgets stay unchanged. `beforeEach` uses the same reviewed minimum on macOS/Linux and stays 90 seconds elsewhere; `afterEach` remains 30 seconds.

The original 33,708-byte file was not exempt from the 20KB rule. Its fixture functions moved to adjacent `intent-publication-dependency-fixture.ts`; the final completion cases moved to `intent-completion-dependencies.test.ts`. Both test files retain the original file-local hoisted state and two mocks. The fixture module adds no mutable module-level state. Vitest forks retain their original isolation.

## Hosted evidence correction and blocking coverage

The b886 workflow inherited `continue-on-error: true` for Intent execution. Job success was not test success: actual macOS Node22 shard2 had six failures and Node24 shard2 had four failures, with test windows exceeded and real controller abort errors. These remain failures. The split also left the five completion cases outside the workflow's single-file collection. The earlier claim of both hosted Intent groups passing is withdrawn.

The workflow now collects, runs and audits both files in separate directories for every OS/Node/shard, aggregates eight distinct file/platform groups (24 manifests), and treats either real runner's nonzero exit as a blocking job failure. Both runs and audits execute independently after successful collection, including after another run fails. The original nine blocking source suites remain. Generic coverage auditing still records failed outcomes truthfully; no test failure is accepted as a release pass.

Independent Kimi approved the correction design and the macOS 300-second runner minimum based on actual hosted costs and multi-fixture cases. Only full fixtures constructed inside the case use it; five invocation cases' existing setup/runner boundaries and every production/SDK clock, signal and join remain unchanged. Job and per-run observation ceilings are 45/30 minutes. No cancellation, unknown close or late error becomes success.

Final b886 local minimum Node22.14 complete two-file execution passed 22 mandatory cases with one original opt-in skip (exit0, 750.88s). This local pass is distinct from the failed hosted attempts. The corrected final complete SHA must execute and pass both files in CI before release.
